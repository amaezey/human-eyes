## A Different Kind of Holiday Visit

For most of my adult life, December meant an airport. No matter where we were living, we flew home for the family gathering: crowded kitchen, familiar arguments, too much dessert, and the same group photograph on the back steps.

This year, we are staying home.

The decision has been surprisingly emotional. Our annual trip was more than a habit; it was proof that distance had not loosened our place in the family. Saying we would not come felt, at first, like choosing convenience over connection.

But the journey has changed. Our family now needs four tickets, and the trip involves two flights each way. We have also become less willing to ignore the environmental cost of flying thousands of kilometres for a short visit. That cost is not cancelled by loving the people waiting at the other end.

So we are trying a new arrangement. We will travel less often, stay longer when we do, and use trains where they are practical. This December, we will cook the family recipes at home and join everyone by video before lunch. We have posted gifts rather than carrying them in our luggage, and the children are making a recording for their grandparents.

It will not be the same. That is the point, and also the loss. Traditions can hold a family together, but they should not prevent us from responding to what we know. I hope this change becomes another form of care: for our relatives, for our budget, and for a climate that every future family gathering will depend on.
