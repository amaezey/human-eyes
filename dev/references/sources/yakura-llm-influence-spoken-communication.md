## Summary

A Max Planck Institute for Human Development preprint measuring whether words ChatGPT prefers when editing human text later increase in unscripted human speech. The word list itself is produced from written prose: a per-word GPT score is the log-odds ratio of document frequency between human-written texts and their GPT-edited counterparts, aggregated across seven corpora, three rephrasing prompts, and three pre-GPT-4o chat models. Those scores are then tested against 737,083 hours of English podcast conversation from 824,634 spontaneity-screened episodes spanning 2017 to April 2026, using synthetic-control matching plus Bayesian change-point regression anchored to the 2022-11-30 ChatGPT release, with in-space and in-time placebo tests. A preregistered experiment (N = 496) supplies the individual-level mechanism. For human-eyes the paper contributes two things. First, a complete, ranked, verbatim word list: the 20 highest-scoring GPT-preferred words, the 36 words in the top 1% of GPT scores, and a per-word post-release slope for each, which is directly actionable against `AI_VOCABULARY` in `grade.py`. Second, a dated measurement that the same vocabulary is entering spontaneous human production, plus a reversion for some words after mid-2024, both of which bear on how long any word-list check keeps separating generated from ordinary prose. The corpus is English-only, drawn from a self-selected public-facing podcast population, and the causal window covers the first 18 months after release.

## Main insights

- The word list's provenance is written prose, not speech. The GPT score compares human-written texts against the same texts after a model was asked to polish, improve, or clarify them. Speech is the outcome the paper tests, not the source of the list.
- The 20 top GPT-preferred words by combined reference score, in the paper's own rank order: `delve`, `underscored`, `comprehend`, `bolster`, `boasts`, `inquiry`, `swift`, `meticulous`, `pinpoint`, `groundbreaking`, `surpassed`, `swiftly`, `craft`, `intricate`, `heightened`, `lessen`, `scrutinized`, `discerning`, `necessitated`, `showcase`.
- Appendix A ranks the same set numerically: `delve` alone above log-odds ratio 4, then `underscore`, `comprehend`, `bolster`, `boast`, `inquiry`, `swift`, `meticulous`, `pinpoint`, and `groundbreak` above 2.5.
- Figure 3A prints the twelve largest credible changes as `delve`, `showcase`, `groundbreaking`, `innovative`, `boast`, `intricacies`, `meticulous`, `surpassed`, `alongside`, `hinges`, `crucial`, and `underscore`. Table S2 and Figure S2 name all 36 words in the top 1% of GPT scores with their per-word post-release slope. Twenty-eight of the 36 rose after the release, 13 credibly so; eight fell, two credibly.
- The effect is graded by score, not vocabulary-wide: mean post-release slope is +0.030 in the top 1%, +0.025 in the top 2%, +0.015 in the top 5%, +0.013 in the top decile, and +0.004 across the whole 3,535-word vocabulary.
- `delve` in Science and Technology podcasts ran about 44% above its synthetic control over months 13 to 18 after release, with a placebo p of 0.010; Education +32%, Business +31%, category-agnostic +9%, Sports -7%.
- The rise did not hold. The gap peaked around mid-2024 and then fell below baseline: `delve` at -15% in Science and Technology, -11% in Education, -30% in Business, and -35% in the category-agnostic sample. The turn coincides with the free release of GPT-4o and with public awareness of `delve` as an LLM signature.
- `boast`, `meticulous`, and `showcase` kept rising while `delve` and others reverted.
- The generator itself moved. The `delve` odds ratio in revised arXiv abstracts falls from roughly 380:1 under GPT-3.5-turbo to 100:1 for GPT-4-turbo and 40:1 for GPT-4o; under GPT-5 most signature words are no longer overused and `delve` sits at roughly 0.7:1, below its human baseline.
- The effect is one-directional. Words ChatGPT disfavours show no matching decline in speech: bottom 1% mean slope -0.002 against a chance range of -0.007 to +0.014.
- Word preferences are robust to the rephrasing instruction. Polish, improve, and clarify give closely similar log-odds ratios for every reference word, with clarify mildest and never reversing a preference.
- Log-odds magnitudes vary substantially by source corpus, and the paper attributes that variation primarily to baseline differences in human word choice. `underscore` is its worked example: humans rarely use it in essays, and GPT revisions introduce it frequently across domains.
- Raw stem counts are contaminated. `swift` sweeps in Taylor Swift, Apple's Swift language, the SWIFT banking network, and NASA's Swift telescope. The paper runs a word-sense audit that keeps only curated in-scope senses and reports that it removes a substantial share of raw matches for contaminated words.
- Among the largest movers overall, `delve` sits at GPT-score rank 1.00 and the three next-highest-ranked risers are `prompted` (0.98), `invaluable` (0.94), and `captive` (0.88).
- Ranking words by how much they moved, without conditioning on GPT preference, recovers a different and mostly irrelevant set: the steepest falls are `pandemic`, `vaccine`, `corona`, and `virus`, and several of the steepest rises are ordinary technical vocabulary (`gpu`, `python`, `vector`, `queries`, `transcript`) whose GPT scores sit mid-distribution.
- The eleven stems excluded as prompt artefacts, because models repeat them in their own responses, are `rephrase`, `polish`, `dear`, `text`, `certainly`, `subject`, `readable`, `clarity`, `enhance`, `version`, and `title`.
- In the preregistered experiment, participants used the chatbot's assigned synonym variant 61% of the time against 17% for the alternative during interaction, 58% against 23% when describing entirely new images after a three-minute distractor, and chose it on 63% of forced-choice trials. The effect held across nouns, verbs, and adjectives.
- Only 15 of 496 participants (3.0%) noticed the chatbot's vocabulary pattern, so the uptake was implicit rather than deliberate.
- The 18 experimental synonym pairs are ordinary counterbalanced word pairs, not AI-preferred vocabulary. Which member was the AI variant was assigned at random, so both halves served as the AI variant across conditions.
- The paper's specificity argument: the top-1% words have no referential connection to language models and sit in a frequency band where natural lexical change is slow, unlike the COVID-19 and machine-learning vocabulary its own corpus also shows moving.
- The reviewed v4 supersedes the v3 figures the library currently cites second-hand. v3's abstract reported 740,249 hours across 360,445 YouTube academic talks and 771,591 podcast episodes and named `delve, comprehend, boast, swift, and meticulous`; v4 reports 737,083 hours from 824,634 spontaneity-screened podcast episodes, demotes the YouTube corpus to an appendix replication, and names `delve, showcase, boast, intricacies and meticulous`.

## Evidence and claims to extract

- **Direct source reviewed:** arXiv:2409.01754v4, the complete 65-page PDF including Methods, Appendix A, Appendix B, the reference list, and the Supplementary Materials with Figures S1 to S20 and Tables S1 to S7. The vector-drawn word labels in Figures 1D, 3A, and 3B were read visually from high-zoom renders of the same PDF.
- **Method and sample:** Two studies. (1) Observational. Per-word GPT score from log-odds ratios of document frequency between human-written texts and their GPT-edited counterparts, Bayesian-marginalised over dataset, model, and prompt. Outcome measured over English podcast episodes 2017-01-01 to 2026-04-30, screened by duration (15 minutes to 5.5 hours), English metadata, speaker diarisation (two or more speakers, four or more alternating turns in a middle 10-minute slice), and a Whisper-large-v3-encoder spontaneity classifier trained on 399 human-annotated episodes (interrater ICC 0.87) at a retention threshold of 3.0 on a 1-to-4 scale, about 90% accuracy. Transcription by WhisperX large-v3, held fixed across the whole time window. Text preprocessed by tokenisation, lowercasing, NLTK stop-word removal, non-alphabetic filtering, a three-character minimum, and Porter stemming, with stems relabelled to a representative surface form. Synthetic control per treated word from a 100-word donor pool, excluding the 20 nearest word2vec neighbours and restricted to the central 50% by absolute GPT score; weights by SLSQP on Gaussian-process-smoothed pre-treatment trajectories; effect estimated as the post-release slope of a Bayesian change-point regression on the observed-minus-synthetic log10 gap; significance by in-space placebo (n = 100) and an in-time change-point date sweep (n = 28 pre-GPT candidates). Four robustness specifications C1 to C4 vary donor selection, pool size, weighting, and the audited-versus-raw counts substrate. 3,535 stems analysed. (2) Experimental. Preregistered 2 group by 2 mode by 12 trial design, N = 496 after four exclusions, recruited on Prolific 2026-04-16 to 2026-04-20; 18 synonym pairs across nouns, verbs, and adjectives; `gpt-4o` chatbot covertly prompted to a canonical variant; outcomes scored by stemmed-span matching on human-verified transcripts; Monte Carlo permutation tests and cluster bootstrap.
- **Direct versus cited evidence:** C01 to C28 and C30 are measured or reported directly by this paper. C29 is a provenance finding established by comparing the reviewed v4 against the v3 abstract and arXiv submission history, not a claim the paper makes. The paper's statement that `delve` already rose in scientific abstracts and peer reviews (part of C27) is inherited from Liang et al. and is marked as such; that paper is not ingested here. The Geng et al. academic-conference-talks corroboration named alongside Appendix B is likewise cited, not measured here.
- **Important limits and counterexamples:** The corpus is English-only and drawn from a self-selected, public-facing population of podcast hosts and guests. The main causal window is the first 18 months after release and treats OpenAI's models as the dominant driver; the paper notes that deployment has since fragmented. The stable-unit-treatment-value assumption is named as the binding one and cannot be guaranteed, because ChatGPT may shift all words rather than only the ones it prefers; the authors argue the differential effect survives residual interference. The experiment used self-identified native English speakers on Prolific in a single session after a brief distractor, so it captures only an early signal. Two directional counterexamples matter for any word-list use: several top-1% words fell rather than rose, and `delve` itself reverted below baseline after mid-2024 while the generator stopped preferring it. A third is the unconditioned view, which shows that large credible movement by itself identifies pandemics and product cycles rather than model preference.
