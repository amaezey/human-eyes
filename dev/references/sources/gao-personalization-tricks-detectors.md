## Summary

This ACL 2026 paper introduces StyloBench, an English benchmark pairing literary and blog writing with LLM continuations that imitate individual authors. It tests seven training-free and two training-based machine-generated-text detectors, then uses latent-feature analysis and controlled perturbations to explain why detector performance can reverse under personalisation. The main contribution to human-eyes is a direct warning that prose features treated as generic human-versus-AI signals can change direction in personalised settings. The paper reports a lexical-diversity reversal and a sentence-dimension reversal that it interprets inconsistently as semantic coherence. The lexical result challenges the current generic explanation for pattern B5, while the broader result reinforces the project's existing rule that aggregate pattern evidence cannot determine authorship. The user queue title, "When Personalization Tricks Detectors", omits the subtitle; this card records the full authoritative title at the supplied URL.

## Main insights

- Average training-free detector AUROC exceeded 85% on parts of general-domain M4 but fell as low as 32.33% on Stylo-Literary. Five of seven training-free detectors had mean Stylo-Literary AUROC below 0.5 in the appendix analysis, indicating systematic prediction reversal rather than ordinary weakening.
- Performance did not move in one direction for every detector. Entropy improved on several personalized conditions while most other methods degraded, so personalization is not a universal penalty with a fixed sign.
- Deep literary personalization through continuous pretraining caused more severe instability than few-shot blog prompting in this benchmark. This is a comparison between two different scenarios, datasets, model sets, and author pools, not a controlled causal estimate of personalization method alone.
- The feature-inversion trap describes a cross-domain reversal: a feature direction that separates human and machine text in general data can separate them in the opposite direction after personalization.
- Direct randomized-text controls support detector dependence on the inverted direction. Detectors separated labels aligned with that direction, stayed near chance on random labels, and generally stayed near chance on an orthogonal direction.
- Lexical diversity reversed in the small Section 4.1 probe comparison, which uses XSum and its continuations for the general condition and 150 Jane Austen segments with Phi-4 continuations for the personalized condition. The token-embedding intrinsic-dimension measure found lower diversity for MGT than HWT in the general probe data, but higher diversity for MGT than HWT in the personalized probe data. It does not establish the direction across all StyloBench authors or generators.
- Sentence-level intrinsic dimension also reversed in the same small probe comparison. The relative human-machine dimension difference changed sign between general and personalised probes. However, the paper says lower dimension indicates smoother coherence, while its prose describes the resulting coherence direction inconsistently: Section 6 says personalised MGT can be less coherent, but Appendix E.3 says MGT coherence is lower in general data and higher in personalised data. The dimension values themselves are clear; the direction of the semantic-coherence interpretation is not.
- StyloCheck uses token-shuffled probe sets to estimate detector reliance on inverted features. Section 5.2 and Figure 7 report that 90% of repeated five-probe correlations exceeded 0.5 and 78% exceeded 0.7, while Appendix D.4 says the mean approaches an upper bound near 0.8. The abstract says 85% correlation and the introduction says correlation exceeds 0.85. The preserved paper does not reconcile these summaries.
- The paper reports that training-based detectors avoid some extreme inversions and often remain near random on personalised data. However, Table 6's DeTeCtive averages conflict materially with the detailed values in Tables 12-14. The separate RoBERTa fine-tuning experiment still directly shows strong in-domain gains, weak cross-domain transfer, and a 71.94 Stylo-Blog AUROC after mixed training.
- The appendix examples preserve machine continuations that contain current human-eyes candidates, but the paper does not analyze those surface phrases as causal cues. A focused surface-only run on the preserved GPT-4o blog example flagged `no-em-dashes`, `overall-signal-stacking`, `no-curly-quotes`, `no-negative-parallelisms`, and `no-copula-avoidance`; it cleared `vocabulary-diversity` at type-token ratio 0.584 and `sentence-length-variance` at standard deviation 7.0. The run was explicitly incomplete because agent assessment and structure evidence were unavailable.
- The literary corpus was cleaned of indentation and line-break artefacts, repeated equals-sign separators, links, and long digit strings because the authors say these non-authorial artefacts may distort detector performance. This is direct support for recording source-specific normalisation, not for adding a prose rule.
- The evidence is English-only, limited to literary and blog continuations of at most 512 tokens, and based on controlled static benchmarks. It does not establish multilingual, code-switched, interactive, mixed human-AI, or live adversarial performance.

## Evidence and claims to extract

- **Direct source reviewed:** ACL 2026 final proceedings PDF, DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1998, all 29 pages including the full paper, tables, figures, formulas, footnotes, limitations, ethics discussion, references, appendices, prompts, and long examples; plus the associated two-page Responsible NLP Checklist.
- **Method and sample:** Stylo-Literary uses seven Project Gutenberg authors, 1,000 human test segments per author, up to 3,000 training segments per author, 512-token segmentation, and LoRA continuous pretraining of Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Phi-4 before 30-token continuation. The 7,000 human test segments are reused across the three generator conditions. Stylo-Blog uses 1,000 Blog-1K posts drawn from a public blog corpus collected in 2004, one to three same-author examples, 30-token continuation, and GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and Qwen2.5-72B. The 1,000 human targets are reused across the four generator conditions. All StyloBench generators use temperature 1. The study compares seven training-free detectors scored with GPT-J-6B as the proxy model, adds RoBERTa and DeTeCtive experiments, uses GPT-2 layer-10 residual activations for feature directions, tests 45 generator-subfield subsets for feature-performance correlation, repeats cross-dataset direction analysis 100 times, and constructs 100 shuffled-token probe datasets for StyloCheck.
- **Direct versus cited evidence:** C01-C10 below are direct benchmark, method, result, example, preprocessing, or limitation claims from this paper. Claims about impersonation, misinformation, copyright, earlier benchmark performance, and prior personalization methods are cited background and remain indirect. They do not support project changes here without separate direct review.
- **Important limits and counterexamples:** Entropy sometimes improves while other detectors degrade; continuous-pretraining versus prompting is confounded by dataset and model differences; the reported 21,000 literary and 4,000 blog examples reuse the same human targets across generator conditions; the blog prompt asks for at least 512 tokens while the method says generation is approximately source length and capped at 512, and no final human-machine length distributions are reported; the lexical-diversity and sentence-dimension results use one small XSum-versus-Jane-Austen/Phi-4 probe comparison rather than the full benchmark; lexical diversity is measured with embedding-manifold intrinsic dimension rather than the project's type-token ratio; the paper's semantic-coherence prose conflicts with its stated dimension interpretation and Table 8 values; all StyloBench generation uses temperature 1 to avoid repetition and encourage diversity; correlation alone is not causation, although randomized and orthogonal controls strengthen the feature-dependence argument; the inverted representation is derived with GPT-2 and evaluated on training-free detectors scored through GPT-J-6B; StyloCheck uses meaning-destroying shuffled text as a diagnostic rather than realistic prose, and its reported correlation summaries conflict; Table 6's DeTeCtive averages conflict with Tables 12-14; the displayed GPT-4o example ends mid-sentence; training-based mitigation remains domain-bound; static English examples no longer than 512 tokens omit multilingual, code-switched, interactive, mixed human-AI, and evolving deployment conditions; and the ethics statement says no personally identifiable information was included and no real individuals were targeted, while the paper studies same-author imitation from public literary and 2004 blog corpora and does not describe the claimed anonymisation or personally identifiable information check.

## Skill-use audit

- **Good use:** Qualify generic human-versus-AI feature claims; design a separate personalized-style robustness lane; preserve reversed cases and detector-specific directions; require register, generator, personalization method, and corpus metadata; treat lexical distribution as conditional evidence; reinforce non-authorship wording.
- **Misuse / overclaim:** Converting any AUROC, correlation, latent direction, lexical-diversity result, or surface example into a single-document authorship verdict or a universal threshold.
- **Unsupported use:** Claiming that every personalized model evades detection, that continuous pretraining alone caused the larger literary drop, that type-token ratio itself reverses, that StyloCheck validates human-eyes, or that results generalize beyond the paper's English literary and blog settings.
- **Underused evidence:** The project has no dedicated evaluation lane for author-style personalisation, no test that measures whether a candidate feature changes sign across conditions, and no validated measure of the paper's sentence-dimension reversal or its intended semantic-coherence construct.
- **Patterns left on the table:** Personalized-style transfer as an evaluation axis; feature-direction stability before promoting distributional checks; semantic coherence as a research dimension only; an explicit warning on pattern B5 that higher lexical diversity can occur in personalized MGT; detector diagnostic probes as evaluation research rather than product checks.
