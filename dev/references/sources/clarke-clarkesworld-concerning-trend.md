## Summary

Neil Clarke reports a sharp operational rise in submissions he classified as spam at Clarkesworld, moving from pandemic-era plagiarism and paraphraser output to a late-2022/early-2023 surge associated in his account with AI chatbots. The post supplies two paired text images, two author-produced monthly bar charts, operational percentages and closures, cautions about detector error and evasion, and concerns about costs and barriers for new and international writers. It does not disclose the stories, identification rules, raw counts behind every claim, model information, or validation method, so it supports dated editor-observation, provenance, workflow-harm, and false-positive context—not any prose tell, detector threshold, current prevalence estimate, causal mechanism, or authorship verdict.

## Main insights

- Clarke documents plagiarism, author-name replacement, and paraphraser output before the chatbot-related spike, with one paired 2021 example.
- His first graph and alt text report banned/spam cases typically below 20 per month before reaching about 25 in November 2022, 50 in December, over 100 in January 2023, and nearly 350 by 2023-02-15; no raw table or category definition is published.
- Clarke deliberately withholds the patterns and submission data used to classify AI spam because disclosure could aid evasion and because some patterns, including regional trends, could implicate legitimate writers.
- He reports both false negatives and false positives in third-party tools and acknowledges that his own process may miss generated submissions or reject uncertain cases cautiously.
- The source treats submission-channel exposure, editorial cost, response time, feedback capacity, and access barriers as operational effects; it does not reveal fiction craft defects.
- The 2023-02-20 update reports more than 50 submissions before noon, temporary closure, and a refreshed graph whose final bar rises just above 500 without an exact label or data table.
