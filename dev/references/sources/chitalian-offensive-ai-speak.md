## Summary

This repository packages an opinionated `avoid-ai-speak` agent skill and two blocklists. It groups literal phrases, structural patterns, formatting habits, agent-chat residue, coding-assistant residue, and a seven-step rewriting procedure, then cites upstream catalogues as its source lineage. It contributes a broad practitioner inventory and exposes an agent and coding-assistant register that human-eyes does not currently treat as a dedicated genre. It supplies no corpus, thresholds, evaluation, or independent evidence, so its bans, severity tiers, reader-perception claims, and model attribution cannot set project rules. The update produces a moderate inventory and scope comparison but only a small change in evidentiary understanding.

## Main insights

- The repository is useful as lineage and candidate inventory, not as independent support for any prose tell, threshold, severity, or authorship inference.
- Its claim that readers recognise the listed constructions instantly, lose trust, and read the edited result as human-written exceeds the evidence supplied in the repository.
- It says both that no single banned word proves anything and that every listed item should be cut. Human-eyes already follows the first principle through signal stacking and contextual treatment, while rejecting blanket authorship and universal-ban logic.
- The phrase inventory overlaps substantially with live checks for assistant residue, manufactured insight, filler, formulaic openers, vocabulary clustering, hedging, vague attribution, signposted conclusions, and generic conclusions. Focused source-derived probes show material gaps within each family.
- The structural inventory overlaps substantially with negative parallelism, em dashes, triads, staccato, rhetorical questions, dramatic transitions, copula avoidance, tacked-on participles, significance inflation, formatting, and rhythm metrics. The live project applies thresholds and context controls that the source does not.
- The colon-assertion claim is not covered by a live rule and is supported only by an anecdotal `Opus-4.8` observation.
- The largest scope gap is the agent and coding-assistant section. The live assistant-residue check catches selected pleasantries and follow-up offers, but not the section as a coherent register and not most apology, narration, reasoning-leakage, comment, cop-out, or commit-message examples.
- The rewriting procedure broadly agrees with current process guidance on direct claims, structural repair, plain wording, source naming, rhythm, and removing redundant framing. Human-eyes adds stronger factual-preservation and complete-Audit requirements.
- The source list is inherited evidence. The live library links the likely Max Planck study to Yakura et al., arXiv `2409.01754v3`, through the reviewed Fairbanks card and an indirect citation in the Kobak snapshot. The project now holds a direct card at v4, `yakura-llm-influence-spoken-communication.md`, and C17 records that the inherited `more than 50 per cent` figure overstates the paper's per-category result.
