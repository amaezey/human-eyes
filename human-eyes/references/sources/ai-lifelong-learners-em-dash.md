## Summary

Lily Chambers's practitioner guide catalogues phrase, structure, vocabulary, tone, content, and social-context cues that she associates with LLM-assisted prose, then spends substantial space limiting those cues: models and prompts change, human writing can share them, clusters and context matter, and an em dash is legitimate punctuation. Its contribution is a broad manual-review and editing checklist, not measured detector evidence. The preserved source supplies no sample, comparison corpus, prompt protocol, model versions, frequency table, or cited upstream study, so it cannot justify a standalone authorship verdict, a severity tier, or a numeric threshold.

## Main insights

- The source's most defensible contribution is its repeated instruction to interpret cues in clusters, abundance, genre, audience, personal baseline, and stakes rather than treating one token as proof.
- Its concrete phrase list overlaps live checks for filler, formulaic openers, AI-vocabulary clustering, triads, listification, tonal uniformity, neutrality collapse, faux specificity, and sycophantic assistant residue, but several named variants are not implemented.
- It identifies gaps that the previous card blurred into adjacent patterns: prompt restatement, apology and guardrail macros, nested-list awkwardness, Oxford-comma and contraction cautions, intensifier stacks, modal hedge stacks, dictionary openings, unnaturally neat exemplars, peppy calls to action, and personal social-baseline shift.
- Its positive editing advice is partly useful but needs a factual boundary. Audience fit and read-aloud review are safe process guidance; inventing anecdotes, sensory detail, or opinions to appear authentic would conflict with the project's closed-source preservation rules unless the writer supplies those facts.
- Its em-dash discussion challenges any categorical reading of the live `no-em-dashes` rule. The source is too weak to settle that policy alone, but it is direct practitioner counterevidence that deliberate human punctuation and genre must remain visible.
