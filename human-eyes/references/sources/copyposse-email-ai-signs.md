## Summary

Alex Cattoni presents five practitioner watch prompts for marketing email: inflated or cliche claims, repetitive formulae, absent personal storytelling, stiff transitions and formality, and grammar that feels too polished for the intended conversational register. The complete current article and its media materially expand the previous two-feature card. The page supplies examples and the author's own human-written counterexamples, not a model study, prevalence measurement, validated detector, or authorship method. Its strongest project value is genre-specific review and false-positive control: it supports contextual review of slogans, hype, lived specificity, and transition register while directly resisting punctuation-, grammar-, and single-feature verdicts.

## Main insights

- The article's five-part taxonomy is complete, but every prose-feature claim is practitioner observation without a disclosed corpus, model/version, comparison group, coding method, rate, or threshold.
- Its seven quoted slogan examples make repetitive three-beat rhythm concrete; the live surface checker instead catches the quote sequence as staccato/anaphora and finds other grammatical triads, showing that feature recognition and the author's category are not identical.
- The storytelling section supplies two preserved human examples grounded in identifiable events and work history. They illustrate source-grounded specificity, not measured performance or a safe instruction to invent personal detail.
- The transition section names “Furthermore,” “In conclusion,” and “That being said,” then explicitly treats the last phrase as legitimate in context.
- The grammar section treats fragments, parentheses, bolding, ellipses, uppercase, invented words, and even an em dash as deliberate email voice choices. This is useful human-look-alike evidence and qualifies blanket cleanup.
- The Bynder screenshot supports only a cited perception figure. The direct [Bynder review](bynder-ai-marketing-study.md) shows why it cannot validate Copy Posse's prose-feature list.
- The embedded WSU video preserves cited skepticism, a brand-perception null result, and transparency figures; it does not connect any survey response to the five signs.
