## Summary

Originality.ai's living vendor page markets a commercial AI-text classifier and related content-quality tools. The complete 2026-07-17 capture preserves the page's six-column study comparison, current model list, score and false-positive explanations, 100-word minimum, multilingual and paraphrase claims, AI Allowance, Deep Scan, sentence highlighting, writing replay, document history, reports, testimonials, press excerpts, and FAQs. Its strongest project-relevant material is the page's own caution to use detection as one signal, retain a human reviewer, examine writing history and drafts, avoid disciplinary decisions from a score alone, and treat scores as probability rather than guilt. Those are vendor statements, not independent validation. The page supplies no prose tell, human-eyes rule evidence, reproducible pooled benchmark, calibrated authorship probability, or basis for transferring its product metrics, highlighting, or rewrite-to-score workflow into human-eyes.

## Main insights

- The current page newly gives prominent cautionary wording: use AI detection as one signal rather than a final decision, inspect writing history and drafts before high-consequence decisions, keep a human reviewer in the loop, and do not use a score alone for academic-integrity discipline.
- It presents scores as probabilities of `Likely AI` or `Likely Original`, explicitly says probability is not guilt, acknowledges false positives, says shorter text is less accurate, and imposes a 100-word minimum. These are product-specific statements, not proof that the displayed percentages are calibrated probabilities across contexts.
- The six cited studies report different metrics and tasks: accuracy on AH&AITD, percentage correct for GPT-4 papers, mean accuracy at a 5% false-positive rate on non-adversarial outputs, mean accuracy on ChatGPT and rephrased articles, mean AUROC across several oncology-abstract contrasts, and mean accuracy for human-versus-AI and human-versus-disguised student text. The page places them side by side but they are not one comparable pooled benchmark.
- The current page advertises `99%` accuracy on latest models and `97.8%` overall accuracy for a 30-language multilingual model. The linked first-party pages supply version- and dataset-specific context, but the target page itself does not bind those claims to a complete reproducible method or independent audit.
- AI Allowance, sentence highlights, the revised Deep Scan wording, editing and rescanning expose useful product-landscape ideas but also risk detector-directed camouflage and meaning drift. Human-eyes' closed-source preservation and no-score/no-authorship boundaries are stronger safeguards.
- Writing replay, session history, document history, authors, durations, character counts, scan history, and shareable reports shift review toward process evidence. They may help a writer rebut a false accusation, but a product-generated history or absence of history does not prove authorship.
- The prior page exposed Lite, Academic, Turbo, and Multilingual mode cards, mode-specific false-positive rates, a 95% paraphrase claim, a confusion-matrix explanation, modified BERT/RoBERTa wording, and a categorical treatment of AI editing. Those details are absent or materially rewritten on the current target page and are retired from the current authoritative claim set rather than silently carried forward.
