# human-eyes

A [Claude Code skill](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/agents-and-tools/claude-code/skills) for auditing and rewriting prose that carries common AI-writing tells.

human-eyes is a pattern detector, not an AI detector. It flags specific patterns in text that statistically give readers the 'AI ick' when used without intent.

Inspired by [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer), built around a deterministic grader, an iteration harness, and a corpus of matched human and AI essays for testing.

## Why this exists

Much of the text we consume and create today is generated by AI. While there are some obvious signs of AI writing, there are subtler tells that are harder to diagnose and repair. The effect is cumulative: AI fingerprints on the voice, rhythm, structure, and phrasing all compound to give you the AI ick.

The patterns aren't the problem. Good writers use triads, em dashes, and contrast framing on purpose. AI uses them without intent. human-eyes views AI writing as inferior to human writing but accepts the reality that it is here to stay. Rather than detecting authorship (like commercial AI detectors) or rewriting blind (like most AI writing skills), it audits a catalogue of research-backed and house-style patterns and gives you options to act on each flag.

Surface patterns are detected by a deterministic Python script. Structural, referential, tonal, and content-shape problems are assessed against a fixed semantic registry. This reduces agent improvisation, and the grader rejects incomplete or stale semantic readings.


## What it does for you

When you invoke the skill and provide text, human-eyes runs a complete **Audit** first: a summary block plus every flagged pattern with exact evidence from the draft. Auto-detected patterns appear first, followed by agent-assessed findings.

The audit ends with a prompt: full coverage report, suggestions, rewrite, or save. Each runs only when asked.

- **Full coverage report**: the same audit plus coverage tables for every check, both auto-detected and agent-assessed.
- **Suggestions**: alternative phrasing for each flag.
- **Rewrite**: rewrites the draft, checks facts and references, then audits the result again. Balanced fixes surface and strong patterns. All also reworks structural ones.
- **Write**: a fresh draft to a brief, with the patterns in mind.
- **Save report**: writes the audit or before/after comparison to Markdown.

## Usage

Invoke with `/human-eyes` or ask Claude to "audit this", "human-eyes this", "unsloppify", "strip the AI tells", or "rewrite to sound human". The Audit action runs first. Suggestions, Rewrite, and Write only run when asked.

### Run the grader directly (no LLM)

The grader can produce deterministic surface-only output without an LLM. A complete Audit also needs answers for every record in the semantic registry.

```bash
python3 human-eyes/scripts/grade.py preflight <file> --work-bundle /tmp/human-eyes-work.json
python3 human-eyes/scripts/grade.py audit <file> --work-bundle /tmp/human-eyes-work.json --format markdown
```

`preflight` writes a bound work bundle containing the semantic questions and candidate spans. Complete every semantic answer in that bundle before running `audit`. For deterministic development output, use `audit <file> --surface-only`; surface-only output cannot unlock Suggestions, Rewrite, or Write.

## How it works

human-eyes has four layers:

- **Pattern catalogue.** A registry of named AI-writing patterns with severity, sources, and before/after examples. Source of truth is `human-eyes/scripts/patterns.json`; the human-readable view is `human-eyes/references/patterns.md`.
- **Grader.** A deterministic Python script (`human-eyes/scripts/grade.py`) that runs regex and density checks against the draft. No LLM in this layer, so the same input always produces the same audit.
- **Agent-judgement registry.** Fixed semantic readings the grader cannot settle with regex alone. These include structural monotony, referential clarity, formulaic parallelism, redundant restatement, underspecified language, context leakage, performed candour, and the existing voice and genre checks. The registry lives in `human-eyes/scripts/judgement.json`.
- **Bound work bundle.** `preflight` binds the source text, catalogue, document structure, candidate spans, and semantic questions with digests. The final Audit accepts the bundle only when every required answer is present and still matches the source.

When you invoke the skill, it creates the work bundle, completes every semantic reading, and asks the grader to validate and render the full Audit. Any generated suggestion or rewrite is checked in context and audited again against a fresh bundle.

## What testing showed

Human-eyes ran across 63 human-written documents and 49 AI-written documents.

- AI documents drew 41% more flags overall than human documents.
- On the serious flags, AI drew 62% more.

### Limits of the test set

- 63 human documents against 49 AI documents.
- 47 of the 63 human documents are from the 21st century. The rest are 19th and 20th century literature and eight older legacy samples.
- 10 of the 49 AI documents are rewrites of human documents that also appear in the human set.
- Cut-offs come from published research on human and machine text. They were then checked against the same 112 documents the figures above measure, so the results are not from unseen writing.

## Where to be careful

- **Patterns drift.** AI vocabulary lists go stale ("delve" peaked in 2023 to 2024). The catalogue needs periodic refresh.
- **The skill itself is an LLM.** It can introduce the patterns it is trying to catch (neutrality collapse, pronoun depletion, generic substitution). The semantic-preservation step in the rewrite flow mitigates this but cannot eliminate it.
- **Detection is asymmetric.** The skill catches AI doing patterns badly. It can also wrongly flag humans doing the same patterns well. The audit voice tries to make this distinction visible; the user has to apply judgement.
- **Sample sizes are small.** Findings here are based on a matched corpus of human, AI fresh-write, and AI rewrite samples (n=5 per group). Treat them as directional at this sample size.

## What's next

Active work, with open questions tracked in `dev/hypotheses.md`:

- Calibrating thresholds by register (literary essay vs corporate doc vs news copy).
- Adding sentence-length mean as a grader signal (variance is in; mean is the cleaner separator in matched-genre comparisons).
- Demoting pattern checks that do not separate humans from AI in matched genres (em dashes and manufactured insight may belong in softer categories).
- Growing the matched corpus past n=5 per group, with bootstrap confidence intervals so weak-signal patterns demote automatically.
- Calibrating severities on the agent-judgement items against the corpus rather than curated guesses.
- Verifying the rewrite step doesn't itself flatten the writer's stance (Abdulhai-style semantic preservation).
- Catching performative-direct fragments ("Direct. Punchy. Inevitable.") as a candidate pattern.
- Closing grader-integrity gaps: catalogued patterns without programmatic checks, and grader checks without catalogued patterns.
- Keep source promotions current as the catalogue changes: promoted pattern additions, hypotheses, process guidance, metadata conventions, and non-promotions are tracked in `dev/references/sources/pattern-opportunities.md`.
